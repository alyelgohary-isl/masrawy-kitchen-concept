What is molokhia?

Molokhia (molekheya) is a green Egyptian soup of finely chopped jute mallow leaves cooked in broth and finished with garlic fried in coriander.

Molokhia is the dish that divides first-timers and converts them. The leaf is jute mallow, chopped almost to a paste, simmered in chicken or rabbit broth, and finished at the last second with ta'leya: garlic and ground coriander fried in ghee and poured in with a hiss. That sizzle is the sound of an Egyptian kitchen.

It is eaten over white rice with vermicelli (sha3reya), or with bread, usually alongside chicken. The texture is silky, which is either the best or the strangest thing about it depending on who you ask.

At Masrawy

Questions
Is molokhia vegan?

The molekheya itself is made with house broth; ask the team about the broth before ordering if you are strictly vegan.

What does molokhia taste like?

Green, garlicky and savoury, close to spinach cooked in a good stock, with a silky texture from the leaf.

Can you cater molokhia?

Yes. Molekheya is on the private catering soups list and travels well in trays.
